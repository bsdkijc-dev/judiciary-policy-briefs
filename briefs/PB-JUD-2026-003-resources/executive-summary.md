# Executive Summary (`PB-JUD-2026-003`)

**Judicial Resources and Institutional Capacity: Analyzing Judicial Staffing, Budget Allocation, and Caseload Density**

- **Publication ID**: `PB-JUD-2026-003` | **Version**: `1.0.0` | **Date**: November 26, 2026
- **Author**: Cecep Mustafa | **Initiative**: Indonesia Judiciary Open Data Initiative
- **Disclaimer**: Independent research; not endorsed by the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (*Mahkamah Agung RI*).

## Key Verified Findings
1. **Constrained Apex Bench Under Surging Demand**: The Supreme Court (`MA-RI`) operated with **48** career Justices in 2023, dipped to **45** in 2024, and returned to **48** in 2025 (below the statutory cap of 60, supplemented by 9 Ad Hoc Justices), even as caseload reached **38,148** in 2025.
2. **Three-Judge Panel Workload Multiplier**: Because Indonesian courts adjudicate in three-judge panels, 2025 caseload translates into **2,384** file allocations per Supreme Court Justice per year, **1,247** per First Instance judge (**7,032** judges handling 2,922,627 cases), and **158** per Appellate judge (**1,226** judges handling 64,377 cases).
3. **High Budget Execution Amid Structural Rigidity**: `MA-RI`'s budget ceiling rose from **Rp 11.90 trillion** (2024) to **Rp 13.15 trillion** (2025, `Rp 13,145,686,448,000`), achieving **98.12%** overall realization (Personnel 98.50%, Goods 97.42%, Capital 96.96%).
