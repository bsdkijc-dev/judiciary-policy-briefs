# Executive Summary (`PB-JUD-2026-006`)

**Digital Justice and Institutional Transformation: Evaluating e-Court, e-Berpadu, and Electronic Appeals in Indonesia**

- **Publication ID**: `PB-JUD-2026-006` | **Version**: `1.0.0` | **Date**: November 26, 2026
- **Author**: Cecep Mustafa | **Initiative**: Indonesia Judiciary Open Data Initiative
- **Disclaimer**: Independent research; not endorsed by the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (*Mahkamah Agung RI*).

## Key Verified Findings
1. **Eight-Year Regulatory & Platform Arc (2018–2026)**: Indonesia progressed from initial electronic civil filing (`PERMA 3/2018`, `e-Court`) and online trials (`PERMA 1/2019`, `e-Litigation`) to integrated criminal case files (`PERMA 4/2020`, `e-Berpadu`), electronic Cassation/PK (`PERMA 6/2022`, `SK KMA 207/2023`), `e-HUM` (`SK Panitera 872/2025`), and `e-Tax Court` (`SK Panitera 1467A/2025`).
2. **99.12% Apex Electronic Adoption**: By November 2026, **99.12%** of Supreme Court cassation and PK appeals were submitted electronically, leaving only **0.88%** manual filings due to regional connectivity/technical limits.
3. **Interoperability & Responsible AI Readiness**: Connecting `SIPP`, `SIAP-MA`, `Direktori Putusan`, and `RESPEK` creates the foundation for structured knowledge management and responsible AI suites (such as `JPAIS` under human-in-the-loop governance).
